# Better Home Philosophy Page — Production Planning v1

*Planning the emotional rhythm, image language, motion pacing, and editorial atmosphere of a cinematic residential web experience.*

---

## Opening Statement

The Better Home Philosophy page is not an interactive product demo.

It is a slow residential emotional journey — one continuous atmosphere experienced through scroll, image, darkness, typography, and near-invisible motion. Production planning for this page is therefore not a question of how many animations to add. It is a question of how to govern viewer attention, emotional pacing, silence, shadow, and visual restraint so the home feels real before it feels explained.

The highest form of immersive storytelling — the kind discussed in restrained cinematic web practice, including Apple’s immersive editorial work at its best — does not win attention through constant stimulation. It guides attention gently. It allows presence. It leaves breathing room for the gaze. It respects emotional rhythm and motion restraint so the viewer remains inside the experience rather than evaluating the interface.

This document is the production handbook for that discipline: scene planning, image planning, motion planning, scroll pacing, typography rhythm, emotional transitions, darkness pacing, and implementation restraint. It pairs with `better-home-philosophy-scrollytelling-script-v2.md` (directorial intent) and Better Home visual governance (what may enter the frame at all).

The finished page should feel like walking slowly through a quiet home — not browsing a technology company’s capability showcase.

---

## SECTION 1 — OVERALL EXPERIENCE RHYTHM

The page breathes in waves, not in uniform density.

**Emotional rise and fall** follows residential time: still dawn, deepening light awareness, lived trace, invisible systems, evening release, night rest. No scene should peak louder than the previous unless the script demands it — and only Scene 05–06 may carry “weight” without carrying “spectacle.”

**Darkness intervals** are scheduled, not leftover. Between scenes and within scenes, dark or near-dark frames reset the nervous system. A page that is always showing something is always asking for evaluation. Better Home asks for inhabitation.

**Visual silence** means frames where nothing new is introduced — hold on shadow, hold on grain, hold on empty corridor. Silence is production content, not implementation gap.

**Pacing hierarchy** ranks atmosphere above information, image restraint above image count, transition calm above transition cleverness, and scroll tempo above scroll distance as a metric.

**Transition density** should remain low. One primary transition method per scene boundary (fade, cross dissolve, or darkness bridge) — not a portfolio of effects proving craft.

**Viewer fatigue prevention** is structural: fewer beats, longer holds, lower motion amplitude, no repeated “surprise” scroll triggers. Fatigue reads as cheapness in residential editorial work.

**Scroll calmness** means scroll maps to emotional tempo, not to section height for SEO or feature disclosure. Fast scroll through this page is a failure mode, not a user preference to optimise.

**Attention guidance** is gentle: composition, light pool, typographic isolation, and darkness direct the eye — not pulsing arrows, not animated callouts, not parallax that shouts “look here.”

**Why excessive motion destroys residential calm:** homes are long-duration environments. Motion that performs for the screen trains the viewer to watch the mechanism. Calm requires the mechanism to disappear. Constant stimulation converts a residential film into a demo reel. Production must treat motion as environmental change — light shifting, opacity resolving, shadow deepening — never as interface entertainment.

Scroll is not navigation. Scroll is emotional tempo.

## SECTION 2 — SCENE-BY-SCENE PRODUCTION PLANNING

The six scenes align with Script v2. Each block below is a production plan, not a copy deck.

---

### SCENE 01 — Before The House Wakes

#### Scene Intent

Establish threshold stillness. The house precedes the person. Viewer enters slowed attention before philosophy is spoken.

#### Emotional Weight

Light — foundational, fragile. This scene sets the contract: calm or nothing.

#### Visual Density

Low. One dominant atmospheric frame may carry the scene; optional one detail insert. Density increases later; here, withhold.

#### Recommended Image Count

| Role | Count | Notes |
|------|-------|--------|
| Hero / foundational | 1 | Partial dawn interior or threshold; no full-room showcase |
| Detail | 0–1 | Curtain fold, timber edge, sill shadow — only if hero lacks tactility |
| Transition | 0 | Scene ends in held light, not new image stack |
| Darkness frame | 1 (opening) | Page may open on near-black 0.8–1.2s equivalent hold before first image resolves |

**Total productive stills:** 1–2 plus opening darkness.

#### Motion Planning

**Allowed:** opacity fade from darkness; subtle dissolve between opening black and first image; imperceptible curtain drift or dust in light shaft (optional, looped video ≤3% movement); shadow edge softening over long duration.

**Forbidden:** aggressive parallax; floating UI; zoom into hero; staggered text bounce; “luxury reveal” wipe; loading-style skeleton animation.

#### Scroll Duration

Longest proportional scroll band on the page (suggested: 18–25% of total scroll distance for ~6-scene page, calibrated in build). Few beats: image hold → first line → second line → hold.

#### Typography Timing

Text enters only after image has been held in silence (production: minimum perceived pause before first line). First line holds 2–3× longer than instinct suggests. Second line follows; no paragraph blocks. Exit via fade with image, not snap.

#### Darkness Planning

Opening darkness hold: emotional reset from prior web context (marketing site, portal chrome). Mid-scene darkness optional only at transition to Scene 02 — brief (brief = still seconds, not milliseconds).

#### Transition Method

Darkness → slow opacity rise on first image. Scene exit: cross dissolve or opacity layering into Scene 02 — no hard cut to bright kitchen.

#### Attention Guidance

Gaze to light entry plane (window edge, curtain, wall wash). Negative space above or beside text. No centre-aligned “hero product” focal point.

#### Emotional Goal

*The day has not rushed me yet. I am inside something still.*

#### Failure Modes

Bright white kitchen hero; staged breakfast; visible tech; HDR flat room; scroll too short; text appears before atmosphere registers; motion visible as “effect.”

---

### SCENE 02 — Light As Atmosphere

#### Scene Intent

Teach through feeling that light is infrastructure for emotion, not a catalog of fittings.

#### Emotional Weight

Medium-light — intellectual calm, not excitement. Viewer deepens attention.

#### Visual Density

Medium-low. Sequence of 2–3 surfaces receiving light, not one explain-all render.

#### Recommended Image Count

| Role | Count | Notes |
|------|-------|--------|
| Foundational | 1 | Establish room depth with layered light |
| Atmospheric transition | 1–2 | Wall wash → bench → edge — sequential stills or slow crossfade set |
| Detail | 1 | Plaster grain, timber edge, joinery shadow |
| Darkness frame | 0–1 | Optional micro-pause between surface beats |

**Total:** 3–4 stills maximum.

#### Motion Planning

**Allowed:** slow cross dissolve between light states; opacity layering; light “drift” via dissolve series rather than literal moving beam; shadow transition on single frame pair.

**Forbidden:** fixture hero rotation; RGB cycle; spotlight sweep; parallax layers > subtle vertical shift; text sliding in from sides.

#### Scroll Duration

15–20% of total scroll. Scroll should feel like light moving across planes — one beat per surface.

#### Typography Timing

Optional single line: *Light is emotional infrastructure.* Appears mid-scene after at least one silent image beat. Hold long; fade before Scene 03 residue imagery.

#### Darkness Planning

Low — use shadow within frames, not full-page black, unless transitioning to Scene 03 (brief bridge acceptable).

#### Transition Method

Cross dissolve primary; opacity layering between stills. Avoid wipe.

#### Attention Guidance

Follow light gradient across frame — composition leads eye along wash path. Typography secondary, lower in frame or isolated in negative space.

#### Emotional Goal

*Brightness is not the point. The room feels considered.*

#### Failure Modes

Showroom evenness; visible downlight grid as subject; tech-demo “scene change”; too many images; copy explains what image should evoke.

---

### SCENE 03 — The Rhythm Of Living

#### Scene Intent

Introduce temporal living through narrative residue — traces, not actors.

#### Emotional Weight

Medium — human warmth without performance.

#### Visual Density

Medium. Alternation of detail and threshold; still restrained.

#### Recommended Image Count

| Role | Count | Notes |
|------|-------|--------|
| Foundational | 1 | Threshold, passage, or quiet occupied corner |
| Detail / residue | 2 | Linen, chair shift, open door — no faces centre-frame |
| Transition | 1 | Between residue beats if dissolve series |
| Darkness frame | 0 | Shadow in-frame preferred |

**Total:** 3–4.

#### Motion Planning

**Allowed:** environmental dissolve sequence; edge blur if figure implied; curtain micro-movement.

**Forbidden:** walking actor; lifestyle stock gestures; swipe transitions; Ken Burns zoom on “emotional” object.

#### Scroll Duration

14–18%. More beats than Scene 01–02 but each beat still held. Fragments map one line per beat.

#### Typography Timing

Fragment lines — one per scroll beat. Long hold between lines. No paragraph.

#### Darkness Planning

Optional 0.5-beat dimming between fragments — opacity reduction, not black screen, unless emotional reset needed before Scene 04.

#### Transition Method

Dissolve between residue frames. To Scene 04: fade to calmer, more integrated space — lower visual noise.

#### Attention Guidance

Residual object first, architecture second in detail frames; threshold frames guide forward motion through house.

#### Emotional Goal

*People live here. Nothing is performing for me.*

#### Failure Modes

Lifestyle commercial; obvious story; clutter staging; too many props; comedy of “perfect imperfection.”

---

### SCENE 04 — Quiet Technology

#### Scene Intent

Prove integration by absence. Systems felt, not shown.

#### Emotional Weight

Medium — quiet confidence. Shortest word count on page.

#### Visual Density

Low-medium. Architecture and light carry scene; technology nearly zero.

#### Recommended Image Count

| Role | Count | Notes |
|------|-------|--------|
| Foundational | 1 | Corridor, volume, or flow — integration implied |
| Detail | 0–1 | Flush joinery, wash, path — only if no gadget read |
| Transition | 0 | Hold longer instead of more images |
| Darkness frame | 0 | In-frame shadow sufficient |

**Total:** 1–2.

#### Motion Planning

**Allowed:** slow fade; minimal parallax if any (≤2% vertical); stillness as dominant “motion.”

**Forbidden:** UI overlays; panel glow animation; screen recording; iconography; “smart home” graphic language.

#### Scroll Duration

12–15%. Confidence through length of hold, not number of assets.

#### Typography Timing

Concept lines appear with generous spacing — main line, then optional fragment list with pause between each word group. Fade out before evening warmth rises.

#### Darkness Planning

None required — transition to evening via warming exposure in imagery, not black cut.

#### Transition Method

Opacity fade into warmer grade for Scene 05 — colour temperature shift, not new effect package.

#### Attention Guidance

Architecture lines, light path, material. Viewer should not search for device.

#### Emotional Goal

*The home works. I do not know how, and I do not need to.*

#### Failure Modes

Panel close-up; blue LED; app on phone; feature list on screen; copy longer than image silence.

---

### SCENE 05 — Evening Calm

#### Scene Intent

Arrival and release — warmth pools where life happens.

#### Emotional Weight

Heavy in emotional terms, not in visual loudness. Slower, warmer, denser feeling.

#### Visual Density

Medium. One strong evening frame may suffice; second optional.

#### Recommended Image Count

| Role | Count | Notes |
|------|-------|--------|
| Hero / foundational | 1 | Living edge, seated light pool, partial room |
| Detail | 0–1 | Fabric, timber, wash edge |
| Transition | 1 | Dissolve toward darker grade for Scene 06 |
| Darkness frame | 0 | Peripheral darkness in-frame |

**Total:** 2–3.

#### Motion Planning

**Allowed:** slower dissolve; longer fade durations; subtle warmth grade shift.

**Forbidden:** entertainment lighting; party staging; chandelier sparkle animation; fast parallax.

#### Scroll Duration

15–18%. Deceleration begins — fewer scroll inputs per emotional change.

#### Typography Timing

Minimal or zero new lines. If lines appear, one fragment only. Hold through fade.

#### Darkness Planning

Peripheral shadow deepens; prepare viewer for night path without jump scare.

#### Transition Method

Cross dissolve to lower-key imagery. Opacity fade on typography before image handoff to Scene 06.

#### Attention Guidance

Light pool as emotional centre — seating zone, not TV wall.

#### Emotional Goal

*I am received by this house.*

#### Failure Modes

Luxury entertaining cliché; RGB; over-bright night interior; dramatic orange grade.

---

### SCENE 06 — The Home Resting At Night

#### Scene Intent

Close the journey — pathway calm, darkness honoured, manifesto whisper, fade to black.

#### Emotional Weight

Heaviest stillness. Ending must land in silence.

#### Visual Density

Low. One path image; optional second hold; ending darkness is content.

#### Recommended Image Count

| Role | Count | Notes |
|------|-------|--------|
| Foundational | 1 | Corridor / stair / path — low pathway light |
| Detail | 0 | Avoid over-explaining |
| Transition | 0 | Ending is fade, not new scene |
| Darkness frame | 1–2 (closing) | Final fade to near-black; hold before scroll end |

**Total:** 1–2 stills plus closing darkness.

#### Motion Planning

**Allowed:** slowest fades on page; optional 1–2% path light shimmer; final opacity to black over extended duration.

**Forbidden:** security-flood aesthetic; motion that increases alertness; CTA bounce; logo sting.

#### Scroll Duration

12–15% but with longest time-per-pixel — user moves slowly because there is little left to “get.”

#### Typography Timing

Manifesto lines — one sentence, optional second. Appear after path image held. Fade before or with final black. No slogan typography. No exclamation.

Direction:

*A thoughtful home does not ask for attention.*  
*It quietly takes care of the people inside it.*

#### Darkness Planning

Critical. Final 10–15% of scroll experience may be darkness-dominant. Hold black — editorial full stop.

#### Transition Method

Fade to black only. No bounce back to bright hero on scroll end.

#### Attention Guidance

Path light on floor or stair — low in frame; darkness above. Text centred or lower third with vast negative space.

#### Emotional Goal

*I could sleep here. The house understands night.*

#### Failure Modes

CCTV look; neon path; horror dark; conversion CTA; restarts bright loop; motion draws attention at ending.

## SECTION 3 — IMAGE PLANNING SYSTEM

The page does not need a hero render per section. It needs a **typed image system** governed by editorial pacing.

**Foundational images** establish spatial and emotional contract for a scene — one strong frame preferred.

**Atmospheric transitions** connect light states or times of day within a scene — 1–2 dissolves, not a gallery.

**Detail frames** supply tactile credibility — timber, linen, plaster, path — without explaining systems.

**Darkness frames** are full or near-full viewport punctuation — opening, between major acts if needed, closing.

**Architectural pauses** are holds on structure and void — silence in image form.

**Emotional rhythm frames** map to Morning Clarity, Evening Warmth, Quiet Paths — governed by visual governance docs.

**Transitional silence frames** are holds where the same image remains — production must schedule “no new asset” beats.

**Round 1 exploration** (`visual-exploration-round1.md`) should yield 1–3 approved stills per emotional state before bulk generation. Reject volume. Approve recognition.

**Estimated page total (planning range):** 10–14 stills maximum including darkness-dominant frames — not 6 × hero stacks. Fewer if one image per scene passes the Better Home test.

Editorial pacing beats visual quantity. A page with twelve perfect holds outperforms thirty adequate slides.

## SECTION 4 — MOTION GOVERNANCE

Better Home motion should feel like **environment changing** — dawn arriving, wash deepening, shadow lengthening, house settling — not like **UI moving**.

**Fade language:** default enter/exit for image and type. Duration long enough to be felt as calm (production benchmark: often 800ms–1800ms depending on beat; calibrate by eye, not by dev convenience).

**Dissolve pacing:** cross dissolve between stills in Scenes 02–05; never faster than emotional readability.

**Environmental movement:** curtain, light drift via dissolve series, shadow shift — amplitude minimal.

**Shadow transitions:** grade shifts toward evening; peripheral darkening — not animated spotlight.

**Opacity layering:** text over image — type fades independently only when necessary; prefer unified fade to avoid “layer cake” UI feeling.

**Temporal motion:** motion implies time passing, not interface responding to scroll for spectacle.

**Stillness:** the majority of scroll distance should be static frames or near-static — motion is punctuation.

**Immersive storytelling discipline (Apple-influenced, non-technical):** viewer comfort and presence precede demonstration. Attention is guided, not hijacked. Motion restraint keeps the viewer inside the home mentally. When motion is noticed, it has failed.

Motion must serve atmosphere. It must never prove technical capability.

**Forbidden globally:** scroll-jacking; snap sections; flying layers; zoom explosions; startup stagger patterns; Lottie celebration; parallax addiction; scroll-triggered gimmick chains; “interactive for interactive’s sake.”

## SECTION 5 — TYPOGRAPHY RHYTHM

Text on this page is **emotional timing**, not information architecture.

**Text delay:** copy follows atmosphere, never leads it. Silence before first line in each scene is required production time.

**Long holds:** lines remain longer than web norms. If it feels slow in the editing room, it may be correct on the page.

**Silence before copy:** schedule explicitly in production timeline — “no text seconds 0–4 of scene hold.”

**Single-line pacing:** one thought per beat. No paragraphs in scroll flow.

**Emotional spacing:** line breaks are breath — not bullet lists.

**Restraint in word count:** Script v2 lines are near maximum; do not add marketing elaboration.

**Typography as atmosphere:** display face (Outfit or equivalent) large, light weight feeling, generous letter-spacing on small caps labels if used. Body copy absent or minimal. Contrast low on dark scenes — warm off-white, not pure white blast.

Text is not a block to skim. It is a line to hear.

---

## SECTION 6 — DARKNESS AS PACING

Darkness is not empty loading space. It is **editorial punctuation**.

**Darkness pauses** between scenes reset emotional state — especially before Scene 01 image and after Scene 06 text.

**Emotional reset** prevents the page from becoming one continuous mid-grey feed. Black or warm black (#0a0908–#0f0e0c range, governance-aligned) is valid content.

**Shadow immersion** happens inside frames; **darkness immersion** happens between beats — full viewport.

**Transition silence** means no new asset, no new line — only dark or held image.

**Visual breathing room** reduces nervous system load. Advanced editorial pacing dares to stop. Production schedules stops; implementation does not fill them with spinners or logos.

**Production rule:** if a scene boundary feels “awkward,” lengthen darkness 20% before adding another image.

Darkness is calm when it is warm, intentional, and brief-to-medium in duration — not flicker, not strobe, not accidental unstyled body background.

## SECTION 7 — IMPLEMENTATION RESTRAINT

Implementation must be invisible. The visitor should never think *this is a well-built website* before thinking *this is a calm home*.

**Choices that destroy Better Home:**

Too much motion — multiple animated properties per scroll pixel. Too many layers — parallax stacks, floating text, video + still + grain overlay competing. Over-triggered animations — every scroll threshold fires a new effect. Scroll gimmicks — pinned hijacking, horizontal detours, progress gamification. Parallax addiction — depth for depth’s sake. Over-designed transitions — unique effect per scene breaks editorial unity. Trying to impress stakeholders with “cinematic web” visible as craft. Apple copycat behaviour — borrowing spectacle without borrowing restraint.

**Implementation ethos:**

CSS transitions and opacity first. IntersectionObserver for reveal, once per element, unobserve after reveal. `prefers-reduced-motion` removes transform and parallax entirely. No heavy animation libraries unless a single dissolve cannot be achieved otherwise — default: no library.

Better Home is not tech spectacle. Implementation is the crew, not the cast.

**Portal relationship:** editorial page may use `EditorialShell` (no sidebar) — do not reintroduce portal chrome mid-experience. Return link minimal at end only.

## SECTION 8 — EMOTIONAL REVIEW PROCESS

Review is **emotional QA**, not UI QA alone.

**Process:**

1. **Silent scroll** — reviewer scrolls without reading copy first; notes whether atmosphere holds.  
2. **Darkness audit** — are pauses intentional? Any accidental flash?  
3. **Motion audit** — close eyes, open on scroll — is motion heard before felt? If yes, reduce.  
4. **Tempo audit** — forced fast scroll test; if story collapses, beats are too thin — lengthen holds.  
5. **Performative audit** — anything that feels like “look at this website” fails.  
6. **Home test** — does it still feel like a residence, not a brand activation?  
7. **First reaction test** — spoken aloud by reviewer without prompting.

**Questions:**

Does the page breathe?  
Does scroll feel rushed at any point?  
Does darkness feel calm?  
Does motion feel invisible?  
Does the user feel settled?  
Does anything feel performative?  
Does it still feel like a home?

**Core failure signal:** the user comments on animation itself — *smooth*, *cool*, *interactive* — motion has failed.

**Pass signal:** the user comments on feeling — *calm*, *warm*, *I’d like to live like this* — production has succeeded.

Reviewers should include at least one person unfamiliar with the tech stack to prevent implementation bias.

---

## SECTION 9 — THE BETTER HOME EXPERIENCE TEST

The page succeeds or fails as a whole — not per scene trophy.

**Wrong success signals:**

Nice animation. Cool effects. Premium smart home. Impressive website. Cinematic flex. “You should see this on desktop.”

**Right success signals:**

Emotional calm. Residential warmth. Quiet intelligence. Thoughtful atmosphere. The sentence: *I would like to live like this.*

Better Home should not feel like a technology company showing capability.

It should feel like entering a thoughtfully designed living environment that quietly understands human life.

When production planning, image selection, motion, typography, darkness, and implementation restraint align — the page becomes a director’s cut of the philosophy already written in standards and governance. When they do not align, no amount of frontend polish recovers the emotional truth.
