# Better Home Philosophy Scrollytelling Script v2

*A cinematic residential journey through light, rhythm, calm, and thoughtful living.*

---

## Opening Statement

This page does not explain what Better Home is.

It allows the visitor to feel what Better Home believes a home should be.

This is not feature storytelling. It is emotional residential storytelling — a sequence of atmospheres, not a sequence of capabilities. The scroll is not a sales funnel. It is a pace of attention. The frame is not a product stage. It is a room remembered.

The experience should feel like slowly turning through a quiet residential film — pages of light and shadow, morning to night, architecture before apparatus, life before demonstration.

Whoever directs, designs, photographs, generates, or animates this page should treat this document as a director’s handbook: scene by scene, emotional state by emotional state, with silence as important as image and darkness as important as copy.

---

## Overall Structure

The page comprises six scenes.

Each scene below includes:

- **Scene Purpose** — why this chapter exists in the journey  
- **Emotional State** — the feeling we are building toward  
- **Spatial Feeling** — how the home should be understood in space  
- **Visual Atmosphere** — tone, light, material, density  
- **Motion Direction** — how movement should behave (on screen and in scroll)  
- **Scroll Rhythm** — how fast or slow the viewer should be asked to move  
- **Image Direction** — what to show, how to frame, what to withhold  
- **Text Treatment** — how words appear and how little is enough  
- **Transition Feeling** — how one scene gives way to the next  
- **Sound Feeling** — the acoustic character even if the web page is silent  
- **What Must Be Avoided** — explicit rejection criteria  

The six scenes follow residential time: before wake, light as atmosphere, rhythm of living, quiet technology, evening calm, the home at rest.

## SCENE 01 — Before The House Wakes

### Scene Purpose

To establish that the home exists in time before human performance begins. The house is not waiting to be sold. It is already breathing — slightly ahead of the people who will enter it.

This is the threshold chapter. Everything after assumes the viewer has slowed down here.

### Emotional State

**Morning Clarity** — not bright optimism, not luxury breakfast theatre. Clarity as stillness: the mind could think in this space before the day asks for speed.

### Spatial Feeling

A room or passage in pre-activity quiet. Air implied. Architecture legible but not fully explained. The sense that light has arrived before sound, before conversation, before machines.

The house wakes a little earlier than its occupants — not as metaphor alone, but as visible atmosphere: soft shadow, cool-warm edge at a window, material surfaces holding dawn without glare.

### Visual Atmosphere

Low contrast. Warm darkness at the edges. Soft morning entering — curtain weight, linen tone, plaster or timber catching first direction. Shadow depth in corners; nothing eliminated for “clarity.” Breathing silence: large areas of near-nothing before the first line of text.

No visible technology. No panels, no screens, no indicators. The home is environmental, not operational, in this frame.

### Motion Direction

Extremely slow fade — as if air is gradually becoming visible, not as if content is loading. No pop, no slide, no bounce. If anything moves, it is almost imperceptible: curtain fibre, dust in a shaft of light, shadow edge softening over seconds, not frames of excitement.

### Scroll Rhythm

Slow. Generous vertical pause before the first textual beat. The viewer should feel they have entered a space before they are asked to read. Scroll distance per emotional beat should be long — this is not a chapter to rush through to reach “the point.”

### Image Direction

Partial reveal. Window corner, wall plane, threshold — not the entire kitchen or living pavilion. Architecture first. Detail over overview: light on timber grain, shadow under a sill, curtain fold.

Use project-real or AI-generated imagery governed by Better Home visual standards: matte materials, restrained highlights, editorial calm. Reference: architectural silence, Japanese residential atmosphere in pacing (not in pastiche).

### Text Treatment

Few words. Large typographic breath. Lines may arrive one at a time with long holds between them. Opening statement direction (adapt in production, do not marketise):

*Homes should not simply function.*  
*They should support the way people live.*

Typography should feel editorial — Outfit or equivalent display weight with restraint — never startup hero sizing. Text fades in after the space has been felt.

### Transition Feeling

**Darkness → soft light.** The page may open near-black or deep warm grey; the first scene resolves toward dawn without jumping to midday brightness. The transition is inhalation, not revelation.

### Sound Feeling

Even in silence on the web: the acoustic imagination should be near-inaudible room tone — distant bird, fabric stillness, no music swell, no UI chime. If sound is ever added, it is barely there: a room listening to itself.

### What Must Be Avoided

Kitchen showcase framing. Luxury reveal energy. Bright white interior with every surface visible. Staged lifestyle — coffee performance, perfect fruit, smiling occupancy. Smart-home signifiers. HDR flatness. “Good morning” campaign brightness.

## SCENE 02 — Light As Atmosphere

### Scene Purpose

To shift the viewer’s understanding of light from product to infrastructure. Light is not fixtures on display. It is the emotional medium of the room.

This scene teaches without lecturing: the viewer should leave feeling that brightness is not the goal — atmosphere is.

### Emotional State

Layered calm. Intelligent warmth. The sense that someone understood how light falls through a day, not how many devices were installed.

### Spatial Feeling

Surfaces receiving light in sequence — not simultaneously. A wall wash before a bench. A soft edge before a corner. The room has depth because light has hierarchy.

### Visual Atmosphere

Layered ambient quality. Shadow transitions that are gentle, not graphic. Warmth without orange cliché. Soft edge lighting that whispers geometry. Atmosphere over brightness: some areas remain unexplained.

Fixtures, if visible at all, are peripheral, out of focus, or cropped away. The subject is the feeling of light, not the catalogue of sources.

### Motion Direction

Light slowly appearing across surfaces — a dissolve of luminance rather than a switch-on. Movement should read as natural shift (time, dimmer logic, cloud, curtain) not as animation demonstrating a “scene.”

Crossfades between stills may be preferable to literal light beams moving. The eye should feel duration.

### Scroll Rhythm

Like light itself moving through the room — unhurried horizontal or vertical progression tied to scroll, but without aggressive parallax. One surface, then another. Pauses where the frame is only gradation.

### Image Direction

Close and mid frames: plaster receiving wash, timber edge, stair plane, joinery shadow. Low contrast. Restrained highlights. Darkness still present in the composition.

Reference feeling: architectural editorial, slow cinema interior studies, Aesop-level restraint in visual density.

### Text Treatment

Minimal. Possibly a single line or fragment:

*Light is emotional infrastructure.*

Or no text until the end of the scene — image carries the chapter. If text appears, it should be small relative to the visual field, lower on the hierarchy than atmosphere.

### Transition Feeling

From stillness of dawn toward understanding of light as layered system — not brighter, necessarily, but more articulated. The transition should feel like attention deepening, not like a new advertisement starting.

### Sound Feeling

Soft tonal shift — almost imperceptible rise in room presence, like day advancing behind glass. No spotlight “ping.” No tech UI sounds.

### What Must Be Avoided

Visible fixtures as hero objects. Showroom lighting — even, flat, afraid of shadow. RGB colour scenes. Dramatic cinematic spotlight through smoke. Product demonstration framing. “Look at our lighting design” composition.

## SCENE 03 — The Rhythm Of Living

### Scene Purpose

To assert that a home is not a static photograph. It is temporal — morning, passage, pause, return. The viewer should sense life without being shown a lifestyle commercial.

This scene introduces **narrative residue** (see Visual Governance): not story, but traces.

### Emotional State

**Human Rhythm** — temporal intelligence. The home supports sequence, not snapshot.

### Spatial Feeling

Thresholds, passages, chairs that imply recent use, linen with weight, an open door between zones. Movement implied: someone passed through, will pass again. Not empty, not staged — quietly occupied in potential and memory.

### Visual Atmosphere

Lived atmosphere over pristine styling. Slight misalignment acceptable. Imperfection as credibility. Emotional pacing through variety of scale: a detail, then a corridor, then a pause.

### Motion Direction

Subtle environmental motion only: curtain drift, shadow movement, a blur at frame edge if a figure is necessary — never centre-stage actor. No walking-into-frame stock performance.

Scroll may introduce sequential stills with dissolve — morning logic moving toward midday calm without announcing clock time.

### Scroll Rhythm

Moderate-slow. More beats than Scene 01, but each beat still earns a pause. The viewer is walking through the house mentally — not scanning features.

### Image Direction

Slightly moved chair. Linen left behind. Quiet threshold, open passage. Half-used object without narrative explanation. Avoid faces unless abstracted to silhouette or blur at edge.

Composition: asymmetry, negative space, partial reveal. Kinfolk emotional pacing — domestic slowness, not Instagram performance.

### Text Treatment

Fragments, not paragraphs. Possible lines (production to edit):

*Morning clarity.*  
*Movement through the day.*  
*Rest where rest is needed.*

Each line holds the screen alone before the next. Typography large, sparse.

### Transition Feeling

From abstract light to embodied living — the home becomes something used, not only something seen. Transition should feel like crossing an internal doorway.

### Sound Feeling

Barely suggested domestic texture: fabric, distant footfall absorbed by surface, door soft — never door slam, never kitchen chaos. The sound of a house in civilised use.

### What Must Be Avoided

Actors. Lifestyle commercial blocking. Obvious storytelling — “the family returns.” Cinematic clichés: running child, embracing couple, wine pour. Forced loneliness as aesthetic pose. “Look at this mood” composition.

## SCENE 04 — Quiet Technology

### Scene Purpose

To communicate integration without demonstration. Technology has disappeared into the living experience. The viewer should not think “smart home”; they should think “this works the way life works.”

### Emotional State

**Quiet Technology** — intelligence felt as ease, not as interface.

### Spatial Feeling

Architecture first. Flow between zones. Light doing the work that panels would otherwise claim. Space feels coherent — comfort, access, climate, presence as one environment, not isolated devices.

### Visual Atmosphere

Technology almost absent. More light, more shadow, more volume, more material truth. If anything technical appears, it is flush, peripheral, blurred, or implied by outcome (the path is lit, the room is calm) rather than by object.

### Motion Direction

Minimal. Perhaps a slow push along a corridor where the only “tech” is how well the light follows human need. No UI animation. No screen glow. No app overlay on photography.

### Scroll Rhythm

Steady, unhurried. This scene is confidence through restraint — shorter in word count, longer in visual hold. Do not rush to evening; let the absence of gadgets register.

### Image Direction

Reference Better Home project imagery where systems are integrated — or generate under Quiet Technology prompt language. Emphasise joinery, ceiling plane, wall wash, path light — never control panel hero.

### Text Treatment

One conceptual passage, tightly edited:

*Technology should disappear into the living experience.*

Optional secondary fragments:

*Comfort. Light. Access. Climate. Presence.*

*Not isolated devices — but one coherent environment.*

No feature list. No brand superlatives.

### Transition Feeling

From human trace to systemic invisibility — the home is complete without showing the mechanism. The transition toward evening should feel like exhale, not like a new product chapter.

### Sound Feeling

Near silence. Perhaps the faintest hum of a house at ease — not mechanical prominence. If HVAC or system sound exists, it is felt as absence of annoyance, not as proof of equipment.

### What Must Be Avoided

Apps. UI overlays on photos. Gadget hero shots. Smart-home-demo aesthetic. Blue LED indicators. Touch panel close-ups. Server-rack energy in living space. Any frame that triggers “nice smart home” as first reaction.

## SCENE 05 — Evening Calm

### Scene Purpose

The house receives people quietly at night. Not entertainment mode. Not luxury staging for guests. Arrival, release, warmth pooling where life actually happens.

### Emotional State

**Evening Warmth** — layered, human-scale, emotionally releasing.

### Spatial Feeling

Living edge, seating zone, dining threshold — intimate scale. Peripheral darkness. Warmth gathered, not broadcast. The outside world has withdrawn; inside, measured glow.

### Visual Atmosphere

Layered warmth. Emotional release without drama. Darkness respected — corners deep, centre gentle. Light pooling on timber, fabric, plaster. Low contrast with warm bias.

### Motion Direction

Slower than previous scenes. Heavier in the sense of temporal weight — evening gravity. Dissolves longer. Fades deeper. If parallax exists, it is minimal and vertical, not theatrical horizontal slide.

### Scroll Rhythm

Deceleration begins here. Scroll segments shorter in count but longer in dwell time — each frame should feel like staying in one room after coming home.

### Image Direction

Reference evening project photography or generate under Evening Warmth governance. Living room LED layers, warm wash, no chandelier hero. Partial room, light pool as subject.

Avoid entertainment energy — TV glow as hero, party staging, wine commercial.

### Text Treatment

Sparse. Possibly no new lines — let warmth carry. If text:

*Evening warmth.*  
*Light where life gathers.*

Small relative to image field.

### Transition Feeling

From integration to settlement — the day’s effort leaves the body; the home accepts it. Transition toward night path should darken gently, not cut to black abruptly.

### Sound Feeling

Lower frequency room tone. Soft fabric, distant quiet — perhaps a single note of music only if ever used, and then solo piano or strings at whisper level (production optional; default is silence).

### What Must Be Avoided

Luxury staging. Entertainment energy. Dramatic contrast for cinema trailer effect. RGB scenes. Overlit “night mode” that reads as afternoon. Social performance imagery.

## SCENE 06 — The Home Resting At Night

### Scene Purpose

Night is when the home’s care is most intimate: sleep, children, guests, privacy. The final chapter honours darkness and pathway calm. The page ends — not with a call to action, but with a manifesto whisper.

### Emotional State

**Quiet Paths At Night** — safety without surveillance, guidance without intrusion, **Emotional Comfort** as closure.

### Spatial Feeling

Corridor, stair, threshold. Low pathway light. Most of the frame in shadow. Architecture still readable as shelter. The home resting while people rest.

### Visual Atmosphere

Low luminance. Breathing shadow. Silence as content. Final calm — visual density decreases toward the end. Last frames may approach darkness itself.

### Motion Direction

Slowest motion on the page. Final fade into darkness over a long duration. No bounce-back to bright hero. Ending is exhale, full stop.

### Scroll Rhythm

Progressively slower. The last third of scroll distance may carry only one or two visual beats and the closing line. Viewer should feel they are leaving the house gently, not exiting a funnel.

### Image Direction

Pathway light on floor or stair — soft graze. Deep shadow. No security-flood aesthetic. No CCTV framing. Architectural silence.

### Text Treatment

Ending text is not a slogan. It is a restrained manifesto line — one or two sentences maximum. Direction:

*A thoughtful home does not ask for attention.*  
*It quietly takes care of the people inside it.*

Typography fades last. Hold darkness after text disappears.

Optional footer link: return to Standards Portal — minimal, low contrast, not a button screaming conversion.

### Transition Feeling

Fade into darkness — the page completes as night completes. No loop to bright opening without user intent. If user scrolls up, transitions may reverse gently; do not punish with flash.

### Sound Feeling

Silence, or room tone so low it merges with silence. The acoustic equivalent of a door closing softly. Night air. No sting, no logo sound.

### What Must Be Avoided

Security lighting hero shots. Neon paths. Horror darkness. Smart-home blue glow. “Contact us” end card energy. Motivational slogan. Logo parade.

# Editorial Pacing Principles

The entire page must breathe as one editorial object — not six disconnected sections with scroll gimmicks between them.

**Silence between scenes** is mandatory. Black or deep warm grey beats — not loading spinners, not decorative pattern — actual visual silence where the nervous system resets before the next emotional state.

**Darkness pauses** function as chapter breaks. They are not failures of content. They are punctuation in a residential film.

**Image restraint** means fewer frames, held longer. One strong image outweighs five adequate ones. Reject volume.

**Slow transitions** — fade, dissolve, opacity — are the default vocabulary. Hard cuts are rare and must be emotionally justified.

**Emotional pacing** follows residential time: dawn logic → light understanding → lived trace → invisible systems → evening release → night rest. Do not reorder for “feature importance.”

**Avoiding overstimulation** is a production rule, not a preference. If a stakeholder asks for more animation, more copy, more proof — the answer is governance, not accommodation.

**Scroll as emotional rhythm** means scroll distance maps to feeling, not to information density. Long scroll with few beats is correct. Short scroll with many beats is incorrect for this page.

The page should feel like walking slowly through a quiet house — room to room, light changing, no one selling anything.

It should not feel like browsing a product page, a SaaS story, or a startup launch.

**First reaction test (entire page):**

Wrong: *Nice smart home.*  
Right: *I would like to live like this.*

If the assembled page fails that test, no amount of craft in code rescues it. Re-edit the sequence, darken the frames, remove the gadgets, lengthen the silence, and try again.
