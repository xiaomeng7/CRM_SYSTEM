# Better Home Editorial Image Prompt Language v1

*Generating emotional residential atmospheres — not product imagery.*

---

## Purpose

Better Home does not generate smart-home renders.

We generate calm residential emotional atmospheres — frames that could belong to a home someone actually lives in, photographed or imagined with the same restraint we bring to real projects.

The goal of AI image generation in our world is not to show function. It is not to prove capability. It is not to illustrate a product catalogue with better lighting.

It is to express rhythm, warmth, stillness, atmosphere, emotional comfort, and thoughtful living — the same qualities we pursue on site, in standards, and in editorial scrollytelling.

A prompt that ends with “smart home” or “home automation” has already lost the frame.

A prompt that begins with how the room *feels* at a particular hour — and ends with silence in the composition — is closer to our language.

Use this document when writing for Midjourney, GPT image tools, photography briefs, moodboards, proposal imagery, social content, and cinematic references. It is not a cheatsheet of magic words. It is a shared vocabulary for what Better Home images are allowed to be.

---

## Global Visual Prompt Rules

Every Better Home image — whether captured, generated, or directed — should obey the same underlying grammar. These rules sit beneath individual emotional states. They are the non-negotiables.

### Lighting

Light should feel layered and lived in. Warm ambient glow where appropriate, never as decoration for its own sake. Low contrast across the frame. Soft shadows that hold depth. Restrained highlights — no sparkle competing for attention. Darkness is allowed, even necessary. Cinematic ambient quality: the sense that light has been considered for how people move through the day, not for how a camera loves to expose a room.

Write light as environment, not equipment.

### Composition

Prefer partial reveal over total explanation. Leave negative space active — silence is part of the image. Asymmetrical framing that respects architecture rather than centring gadgets. Atmosphere over clarity: a feeling may matter more than readable detail. Architecture first: lines, volume, proportion. When in doubt, choose detail over spectacle — a wash on plaster, a timber edge, a path at night — rather than a wide shot that documents everything.

### Materials

Timber with grain. Stone with weight. Matte surfaces that absorb. Brushed metal where metal appears — never mirror gloss. Warm textures. Tactile calm the viewer can almost sense. Materials should belong to residential life, not to a launch event.

### Mood

Quiet. Restrained. Thoughtful. Cinematic without being dramatic. Editorial in pacing and intention. Emotionally calm — the opposite of visual anxiety.

When assembling any prompt, return to these four pillars before adding scene-specific language.

---

## What To Avoid

The following directions are out of scope for Better Home — in prompts, in briefs, in post-production notes, and in selection criteria.

Flashy luxury. RGB smart-home aesthetic. Futuristic UI overlays floating in frame. Blue tech lighting as default mood. Showroom atmosphere — perfect, unlived, afraid of shadow. Overlit interiors where every corner shouts. HDR photography that kills depth. Glossy renders with plastic perfection. Excessive gadgets as heroes. Cold modernism without warmth. CCTV installer feeling — fisheye, surveillance framing, industrial green night vision. Generic Pinterest luxury — the same kitchen, the same smile, the same marble island.

Better Home is not tech fantasy.

It is human-centered residential atmosphere.

If a generated image could be mistaken for a smart-home advertisement, discard it and rewrite the prompt from an emotional state, not from a product category.

## Emotional State Prompt Library

Each state below includes direction for feeling, light, composition, texture, motion, avoidance, and a restrained AI prompt example. Examples are starting points — adapt them; do not copy them blindly into every brief.

---

### 01 — Morning Clarity

**Emotional goal**

The viewer should slow down. The day has not yet asked for performance. The home offers clarity without glare — a sense that thought is possible before noise arrives.

**Lighting direction**

Early natural light, soft and directional. Low contrast. Gentle falloff across surfaces. No harsh ceiling downlights. Windows implied or partial. Cool-warm balance leaning warm at the edges, never sterile.

**Composition language**

Partial room reveal. Kitchen bench, corridor, or reading nook — not the entire open plan. Negative space above or beside architecture. Asymmetry. One strong plane of light.

**Texture language**

Matte timber, stone or plaster, linen, ceramic. Surfaces that absorb morning light rather than reflect it aggressively.

**Motion feeling**

If animated: very slow fade-in, as if the room is waking before the occupant. Stillness first.

**What to avoid**

Bright showroom morning. Overexposed whites. “Instagram breakfast” staging. Visible smart panels as focal point. HDR clarity everywhere.

**AI prompt example**

*Quiet residential kitchen at early morning, soft natural light through sheer curtain, low contrast, matte timber and stone surfaces, partial architectural framing, negative space, calm editorial interior photography, emotional stillness, no people, no technology visible, cinematic restraint*

---

### 02 — Evening Warmth

**Emotional goal**

Arrival — not activation. The home gathers warmth as the day ends. The viewer should feel invited to stay, not impressed by equipment.

**Lighting direction**

Layered warm lighting at dusk or early night. Ambient wash, subtle accent, no single harsh source. Shadows deepen in corners. Warm colour temperature without orange cliché.

**Composition language**

Living space or dining edge — intimate scale. Light pools where life happens. Architecture still readable in half-shadow.

**Texture language**

Warm fabrics, timber, plaster, soft reflections. Materials that hold light gently.

**Motion feeling**

Slow dissolve from dim daylight to warm interior glow. No sudden “scene change” energy.

**What to avoid**

Luxury showroom evening. Chandelier heroics. Visible colour-changing LEDs. Cold blue gaps between warm zones. Staged wine-and-laughter lifestyle.

**AI prompt example**

*Residential living interior at dusk, layered warm ambient lighting, soft shadows, timber and plaster textures, asymmetrical composition, partial reveal, quiet editorial atmosphere, emotional comfort, cinematic interior photography, no gadgets, no showroom styling*

---

### 03 — Quiet Paths At Night

**Emotional goal**

Safe, calm movement through a sleeping house. Respect for darkness. Care expressed as low light, not as brightness.

**Lighting direction**

Low-level pathway light — floor wash, recessed step, soft wall graze. Deep shadow elsewhere. Night-adapted exposure — never day-for-night brightness.

**Composition language**

Corridor, stair, or threshold. Strong perspective optional but restrained. Figure optional only as blur or absence. Path as subject.

**Texture language**

Stone, timber floor, plaster wall. Materials readable in minimal light.

**Motion feeling**

Gentle forward drift along path, or static frame with implied movement off-frame. Silence between beats.

**What to avoid**

Horror-movie darkness. Neon strips. RGB path lighting. Overlit “night mode” that looks like afternoon. Security-camera framing.

**AI prompt example**

*Dark residential corridor at night, subtle low-level pathway lighting, deep soft shadows, architectural silence, partial framing, quiet cinematic atmosphere, sleep-friendly calm, editorial photography, no surveillance aesthetic, no bright ceiling lights*

---

### 04 — Architectural Silence

**Emotional goal**

Structure before styling. The space breathes through proportion, void, and material — not through objects.

**Lighting direction**

Sculptural natural or artificial light that reveals form. Strong but soft shadow on planes. Contrast controlled — never graphic for its own sake.

**Composition language**

Wall, stair, void, ceiling plane. Minimal objects. Large negative space. Japanese-influenced restraint without pastiche.

**Texture language**

Concrete, timber, stone, render. Honest material at architectural scale.

**Motion feeling**

Almost none — or a slow pan across a single plane. Hold the frame.

**What to avoid**

Furniture catalogue staging. Decorative clutter. Tech visible. Wide-angle distortion exaggerating space.

**AI prompt example**

*Minimal residential architecture interior, sculptural light and soft shadow on plaster and timber, large negative space, asymmetrical composition, architectural silence, restrained editorial photography, no furniture styling, no technology, emotional calm*

---

### 05 — Calm Presence

**Emotional goal**

Stability. The room does not compete for attention. Confidence without display.

**Lighting direction**

Even, soft ambient — neither morning sharp nor evening dramatic. Balanced layers. No focal hotspot on a device.

**Composition language**

Mid-scale room view with breathing room. Chair, window, corner — inhabited but not staged. Implied living without posed people.

**Texture language**

Soft upholstery, matte finishes, warm neutral palette with depth, not flat beige.

**Motion feeling**

Hold. Fade. Optional slow push-in so subtle it is felt rather than noticed.

**What to avoid**

Hotel lobby calm (cold, grand). Empty real-estate photography. Smiling family stock. Feature wall as hero.

**AI prompt example**

*Calm residential interior, soft layered ambient light, matte textures and warm neutrals, negative space, implied human presence without posed figures, thoughtful editorial atmosphere, quiet confidence, cinematic restraint, no smart home elements*

---

### 06 — Quiet Technology

**Emotional goal**

Systems exist. They do not perform for the camera. Technology has disappeared into the living experience.

**Lighting direction**

Same as the room’s emotional state — morning, evening, or night — with light doing the work, not panels or screens.

**Composition language**

Architecture and atmosphere lead. If technology appears, it is flush, peripheral, out of focus, or implied. Never a hero product shot.

**Texture language**

Integrated surfaces. Joinery-quality finishes. No glossy gadget reflections.

**Motion feeling**

No UI animations. No screen glow as focal point. If motion exists, it is environmental — light, curtain, shadow.

**What to avoid**

Touch panel close-up. App on phone. Blue LED indicators. “Smart home” keyword styling. Server rack aesthetic in living space.

**AI prompt example**

*Refined residential interior where lighting feels intentional and integrated, warm layered light, flush architectural details, no visible gadgets or screens, atmosphere of quiet technology, editorial interior photography, human-centered calm, partial reveal, soft shadows*

## Prompt Construction Philosophy

Better Home prompts are built from the inside out — from feeling, not from category.

Do not write: *modern luxury smart home with LED lighting.*

Do not write: *futuristic home automation interior.*

Do not write: *high-tech intelligent house render.*

Begin with emotional state and time of day. Add light behaviour. Add material truth. Add composition discipline. Add mood words that belong to editorial photography — restrained, cinematic, calm — not to product marketing.

**Wrong direction**

*futuristic luxury smart home interior, RGB accent lighting, touch screens, high-tech*

**Right direction**

*quiet residential interior at dusk, layered warm lighting, soft shadows, timber texture, restrained architectural composition, emotional calm, cinematic editorial photography*

The second prompt may produce an empty room. That is often correct. Better Home sells atmosphere before objects.

When briefing a photographer, translate the same structure: state the emotional goal first, then light, then what must not appear. When reviewing AI output, reject images that feel generated before you reject images that feel off-brand — plastic perfection is as disqualifying as a visible control panel.

## Cinematic Motion References

Still images are one frame of a longer editorial discipline. Video and scrollytelling should extend the same language.

**Slow reveal** — content emerges as the viewer scrolls or time passes, never popping.

**Dissolve** — one mood giving way to another, especially day to evening.

**Darkness transitions** — allowing the frame to go nearly black between chapters. Silence is content.

**Gentle movement** — camera drift measured in millimetres per second, or a figure crossing edge of frame.

**Cinematic breathing room** — holds on empty space, texture, or shadow before the next line of narrative.

**Visual silence** — moments where nothing new appears. The viewer’s nervous system settles.

Avoid startup motion, flashy transitions, product-launch energy, heavy parallax, zoom explosions, and any movement that says *watch our capability* instead of *feel this home*.

Motion prompts for video tools should read like direction notes to a cinematographer on a quiet residential shoot — not like UI demo specifications.

## Closing Statement

Better Home imagery should not feel generated.

It should feel remembered.

Like a quiet moment inside a home that already understands the people living there — light where it is needed, shadow where rest is needed, materials that age with dignity, and nothing in the frame asking for applause.

Write prompts that search for that memory.

Reject everything else.
