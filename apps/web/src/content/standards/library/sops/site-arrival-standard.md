**Operational Standards v1 · Library · SOP**

The experience of Better Home begins before the first tool is lifted. It begins when the vehicle stops, when someone opens a door, and when the home receives its first impression of who we are.

This standard defines how we arrive on residential projects — occupied homes, active builder sites, and handover visits alike. It is executable. It is also grounded in our conduct philosophy: calm presence, respect for space, and quiet professionalism.

---

## Opening philosophy

Clients and builders often decide whether a day will feel easy long before technical work is reviewed. A rushed arrival, blocked access, loud voices in a hallway, or a chaotic unload tells a story Better Home does not want told.

Arrival is not logistics alone. It is the first act of delivery. It shapes trust, homeowner comfort, builder perception, and the emotional temperature of the site. We treat it with the same seriousness as rough-in quality or commissioning discipline.

---

## Intent

This standard exists to create a consistent arrival experience that is:

- **Calm** — no unnecessary noise, urgency, or visual stress  
- **Organised** — prepared crews, contained materials, clear purpose for the day  
- **Respectful** — of driveways, neighbours, private space, and programme  
- **Professional** — presentation and behaviour matching premium residential work  
- **Human-centered** — awareness that people may be living in or visiting the home  

When followed, arrival should feel unobtrusive and confident. The household or site manager should sense that Better Home understands where they are — not that a generic trade has landed.

---

## Before arrival

**Punctuality**  
Arrive at the agreed time — not habitually early in a way that surprises an occupied home, and not late without prior notice. If delay is unavoidable, contact the builder or homeowner through the agreed channel before the expected window passes.

**Vehicle condition**  
Vehicles reflect the brand. Clean enough to park outside a premium home without embarrassment. Load areas organised so unloading is quick and quiet, not a search through clutter.

**Uniform and presentation**  
Wear company-standard attire where required. Identification visible when policy applies. Personal presentation appropriate to a client-facing residential environment — not site-ready in appearance only, client-ready in conduct from the first step out of the vehicle.

**Preparation mindset**  
Review job details, access notes, scope for today, contacts, parking instructions, pet or occupant sensitivities, and any builder-specific site rules before entry. Arrival is not the moment to read the folder for the first time.

**Mental set**  
Enter the day assuming you are a guest in someone’s life environment or a partner on someone’s programme — whichever applies. That assumption governs tone, pace, and decisions for the rest of the shift.

---

## Vehicle and parking standards

**Respectful parking**  
Park where instructed. If nothing is specified, choose the least disruptive option: off the driveway when possible, without blocking garage doors, neighbour access, or delivery paths. Do not use premium frontage as a storage yard.

**Avoiding blockage**  
If the driveway must be used briefly, confirm with the homeowner or site manager. Leave space for emergency access where practical. Move vehicles when asked — without debate in front of the client.

**Premium presentation**  
Close doors gently. Avoid slamming trays or tailgates. Unload with purpose, not performance. Do not leave packaging, scrap, or personal items visible on the street longer than necessary.

**Organised unloading**  
Bring only what today requires when possible. Stage materials at an agreed zone — not spread across the lawn, entry, or living areas. Carts, bags, and cable drums positioned to keep paths clear.

**Reducing disruption**  
Engine noise, reversing alarms, and radio volume should be minimised in residential streets, especially early morning or evening. Neighbours are part of the environment we affect.

---

## Entering the site

**Calm entry**  
One knock. Wait. Do not let yourself in unless access was explicitly pre-authorised. Do not shout from the doorway for someone inside.

**Greeting**  
Introduce yourself by name and company. State who you are here to meet — builder site contact, homeowner, or both. If the client is not expecting you, stop and clarify through the office before proceeding.

**Introducing the work**  
In plain language, explain what will happen today: areas affected, approximate duration, any power or access impacts, and what you need from them (clear zones, pets secured, questions). Invite questions without rushing past them.

**Occupied spaces**  
Ask which rooms are private or off limits. Note children, elderly residents, people working from home, or anyone who may be unsettled. Adjust voice, route, and timing accordingly.

**Respecting private environments**  
Do not photograph, wander, or open doors beyond scope. Treat visible personal life with discretion. The home is not a showroom for commentary.

On builder-led sites, confirm with the site manager where Better Home may work, store gear, and take breaks. Follow their coordination structure — we bring expertise, not a parallel command.

---

## Tool and equipment behaviour

**Organised setup**  
Establish a contained work zone. Tools on protection, not on finished surfaces without approval. Consumables staged, not scattered. A passer-by should see order, not occupation.

**Avoiding clutter**  
Do not colonise hallways, kitchens, or living areas with gear unrelated to today’s zone. Return items to the vehicle or designated storage at breaks when practical.

**Controlling noise**  
Drilling, cutting, and extraction happen with awareness of time and occupancy. Warn occupants when loud work begins. Use the quietest reasonable method for the task. Radios and phone calls at low volume — or off in occupied homes unless the household is comfortable.

**Calm site presence**  
Movement is deliberate. Running indoors is avoided. Doors are closed gently. Conversations with crew happen at normal speaking volume, face to face when possible — not shouted between floors.

---

## Communication expectations

**Tone**  
Steady, courteous, unhurried. No condescension toward clients or other trades. No performative expertise. No complaints about the job, the builder, or the homeowner within earshot of the home.

**Clarity**  
Say what you mean. Confirm understanding. Repeat access or timing changes in writing when they affect others (text or email through agreed channels).

**Confidence**  
Confidence is shown through preparation and calm problem-solving — not through dominating conversation or dismissing concern.

**Emotional awareness**  
Stress is common on active sites and in occupied homes. Do not add to it. If a client is anxious, acknowledge and clarify. Escalate to a supervisor when tension is beyond your role to settle.

**Avoiding chaotic discussion**  
Disputes about scope, sequence, or defects are not resolved in front of homeowners. Pause, document, contact the project lead or builder through proper routes. The hallway is not a tribunal.

---

## What good looks like

A Better Home arrival looks and feels like this:

- The vehicle is parked thoughtfully; the street and driveway remain usable  
- The crew is identifiable, prepared, and briefed on today’s scope  
- Entry is calm; greetings are clear; the day’s work is explained without jargon  
- Zones are agreed; private areas are respected  
- Tools and materials are contained; paths stay clear  
- Noise and voice levels suit a residential environment  
- The builder or homeowner senses order — not another day of trade chaos  
- The first ten minutes increase trust rather than consume it  

Good arrival does not require performance. It requires care.

---

## What does not align with Better Home

The following are incompatible with this standard:

- **Loud arrival** — slamming doors, shouting, horns, blaring audio in quiet streets  
- **Messy unloading** — gear, packaging, and cables spread without containment  
- **Rushing** — moving through the home as if occupancy does not matter  
- **Blocking access** — driveways, paths, or garages left unusable without consent  
- **Chaotic setup** — undefined work zones; trip hazards left for others  
- **Shouting across site** — instructions yelled between floors or rooms  
- **Unannounced entry** — treating access codes or keys as permission to skip greeting  
- **Poor presentation** — vehicles or personal conduct that embarrasses the brand  
- **Dismissive communication** — impatience with questions, builders, or household routine  
- **Visible conflict** — arguments with crew or other trades in client-facing space  

If an arrival would make you uncomfortable in your own home, it does not meet this standard.

---

## Closing standard

Technical excellence cannot compensate for an arrival that erodes trust.

Better Home’s site experience begins when the vehicle stops. Parking, entry, setup, and the first conversation are part of delivery — not preamble.

Hold this standard on every visit: luxury residential projects, renovations, occupied homes, and final commissioning alike. Our Foundation philosophy explains why homes matter. Our On-Site Conduct philosophy explains how we behave in them. **This standard explains how we begin.**
