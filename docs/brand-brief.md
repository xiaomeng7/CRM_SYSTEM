# Brand Brief — BHT (Better Home Technology)

This document defines BHT's brand identity — mission, voice, visual language, and positioning rules. It is the source of truth for all marketing copy, design decisions, and AI prompts used to generate content. Update when positioning changes (see also `docs/marketing-strategy-2026.md`).

## Mission

We design and install electrical and smart home systems that make Adelaide homes safer, more capable, and more livable — and we tell our customers the truth about what their home needs and what it doesn't.

## Vision

To be the trusted electrical partner for Adelaide's premium residential market — the firm luxury home builders specify by name, and the firm home buyers call before they sign.

## Tagline

**"Smart Home & Premium Electrical for Adelaide Homes."**

Sub-tagline:
> Builder partnerships, luxury home integration, pre-purchase peace of mind.

## Brand Promise (One Sentence)

If we don't think you should spend the money, we'll tell you — in writing.

## Core Value Propositions

1. **Independent Honesty.** We give written opinions, not sales pitches. We're paid for advice, not commission.
2. **Smart Home Specialists.** Designed and integrated from the wiring up, not bolted on later.
3. **Local & Licensed.** Mawson Lakes-based, fully licensed, serving all Adelaide suburbs.
4. **Builder-Grade Coordination.** We work with architects, designers, and luxury builders on their schedules and standards.

## Target Customer Personas

### Persona A — Property Buyer (Tier 1.1, B2C)

- **Who**: 30-55, mid-to-upper income, in the middle of a property purchase, due diligence phase
- **Job to be done**: Avoid buying an electrical money pit; satisfy bank or insurance requirements
- **Worries**: "Did the previous owner cover up problems? Will I need a $20K rewire?"
- **Buying state**: Has a contract date in 7-14 days; anxious; price-insensitive within reason ($400-$600)
- **Voice they trust**: Independent, technical, calm, written, fast

### Persona B — Luxury Home Builder / Designer (Tier 1.2, B2B)

- **Who**: Project manager, principal architect, or interior designer at a luxury custom builder (Vogue, Beechwood, Duo, Rendition, smaller boutique outfits)
- **Job to be done**: Deliver a smart home that wows the end client without exploding the schedule or warranty
- **Worries**: "Will this electrician show up on time, coordinate with the tiler, and not embarrass me at handover?"
- **Buying state**: Repeatedly — project after project — once trust is established
- **Voice they trust**: Confident, peer-to-peer, visual portfolio, low-drama

## Tone of Voice

**Confident but not boastful.** We know our trade. We don't shout about it.

**Plain English, not jargon.** Write like a smart friend who happens to be an electrician. Avoid corporate copy ("we strive to deliver world-class solutions"). Avoid tradie macho ("she'll be right, mate").

**Honest to the point of disclosing.** "Here's what this costs. Here's what it doesn't include. Here's what we recommend you don't do."

**Calm urgency.** When a customer needs help, we say "we'll be there tomorrow at 10" — not "URGENT!! CALL NOW!!".

### Voice Samples

✅ **Good**:
- "Before you spend $20,000+ on solar or battery, get an independent written opinion. If we determine it doesn't make financial sense, we'll state that clearly — in writing."
- "Pre-purchase electrical inspection. Booked in 3 days. Written report within 48 hours of site visit."
- "Smart home, designed from the wiring up. We work with your builder and designer from blueprint to handover."

❌ **Bad**:
- "We're the BEST electricians in Adelaide!! 24/7 service!! Call NOW!!"
- "Our award-winning team strives to deliver world-class electrical solutions."
- "Got an electrical issue? No worries mate, we'll sort it."

## Voice Variation: B2C vs B2B

The "Independent Honesty" stance is a powerful B2C differentiator (it's exactly what a property buyer or homeowner wants to hear from a tradesperson). But on B2B builder pages it can backfire — a builder reading "we tell the truth in writing" may worry: *"Will this electrician throw me under the bus in front of my client?"*

**Resolution**: keep the same brand, but flex tone per audience.

### B2C audiences (Pre-Purchase, Energy Advisory, homeowner pages)

- **Lead with**: independent, written, honest, no commission
- **Voice**: "We'll tell you what your home needs — and what it doesn't."
- **Promise**: written reports, severity ratings, "if we don't think you should buy this house we'll tell you in writing"
- **CTA energy**: defensive purchase ("avoid the money pit")

### B2B audiences (For Builders, builder partnership pages)

- **Lead with**: team-aligned, on-schedule, on-budget, no drama
- **Voice**: "We work as part of your team — not against it. Our independent voice is for your client, not against your build."
- **Promise**: fixed pricing, daily reports, two-way overage rate, 3-year warranty, written variation process
- **CTA energy**: low-friction partnership ("request availability for your next project")

### What stays constant across both

- **Plain English**, no corporate jargon, no tradie slang
- **Calm urgency** — "we'll be there tomorrow at 10", not "URGENT NOW"
- **Specifics over superlatives** — concrete numbers, real specs, real photos
- **The brand's overall character**: precise, calm, honest, modern, premium-but-not-snobby

The honesty is real in both cases — it's just **directed differently**:
- B2C honesty = "we'll tell you the truth about *your home*"
- B2B honesty = "we'll tell you the truth about *whether we can deliver your project*"

## Brand Personality

If BHT were a person, they would be:

- A senior tradesperson who runs their own crew, not a sales rep
- The kind of expert who answers your question with the answer — not a question back
- Someone who shows you their portfolio without bragging
- Someone who would rather walk away from a job they can't do well than overcommit

**Adjectives**: precise, calm, honest, modern, premium-but-not-snobby, local.
**Anti-adjectives**: pushy, salesy, generic, cute, cartoony, discount.

## Visual Direction

### Colors

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary (Trust) | `#0E1E33` | Headers, large surfaces, navigation |
| Accent (Energy) | `#F59E0B` | CTAs, highlights, sparingly |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` | Backgrounds |
| Surface-Muted | `#F8FAFC` | Section dividers, cards |
| Text | `#1E293B` | Body copy |
| Text-Muted | `#64748B` | Secondary text |

**Avoid**: cyan `#0EA5E9` (overused in trades), bright greens, pure black, rainbow gradients, the current GoDaddy-era blue/orange/purple gradient.

### Typography

- **Headings**: Geist Sans or Inter — modern, geometric, confident
- **Body**: Inter — readable at all sizes
- **Mono** (for technical / spec callouts): Geist Mono or JetBrains Mono
- **Avoid**: serif "elegant" fonts (wrong category), display fonts that feel cheap, Comic Sans family

### Imagery

- **Real work-site photography only.** No stock electrician handshakes. No AI-generated photos.
- Categories to build a library of: smart home control panels installed, switchboards before/after, EV chargers wall-mounted, CCTV install in progress, tidy cable management close-ups, team in branded shirts on real sites
- **Format**: 4:3 or 16:9, natural lighting, light grading for consistency
- **Avoid**: stock photos, dramatic flash photography, posed "smiling tradie" shots, anything that looks like a Hipages thumbnail

### Logo Direction (when redesigning)

- Wordmark + simple geometric icon (an electrical symbol or abstract mark — **not** a cartoon house)
- Colors: deep navy + amber accent (matches palette)
- Must work in single color (vehicle livery, shirt embroidery)
- Must work at favicon size (16×16) and at billboard size
- **Avoid**: rainbow gradients, drop shadows, cartoon mascots, "BH" letters squished into a lightning bolt

## Brand Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Show real photos of real installs | Use stock electrician images |
| Display License # and Insurance prominently | Hide credentials in fine print |
| Quote prices when fair to do so | Force every interaction through "Get a Quote" forms |
| Write copy that helps customers decide | Write copy that pressures or guilts |
| Match RCG on coverage, beat them on craftsmanship | Try to beat RCG on price |
| Speak the builder's language on B2B pages | Treat builders like B2C customers |
| Lead with "we'll tell you the truth" | Lead with "we're the best" |

## How to Use This Document
